Temporal learning for waste anomaly localization

Extending a hierarchical VQ-VAE-2 with temporal bottom-code consistency, normal-code repair, and decoder adaptation for reconstruction-based anomaly localization.

Ongoing research2026–present
Working draft · PDF (1.7 MB)↗Project repository↗
Normal-only trainingTemporal consistencySingle-frame inference
01 / Question

What I’m investigating

Can information from consecutive normal frames improve anomaly localization without adding a new trainable network?

02 / Approach

How the study is structured

The working framework starts from a trained hierarchical VQ-VAE-2. During adaptation, current-frame bottom codes are encouraged to agree with reliable, detached matches from a previous normal frame. A normal-code frequency rule then supplies an alternative reconstruction path; decoder-only adaptation and bounded RGB blending are investigated as further stages.

03 / Evaluation

What the evidence needs to show

The study examines reconstruction-based localization and the effect of each adaptation stage. Comparative experiments and ablations focus on mean frame IoU, evaluation protocols, and failure cases.

04 / Limitations

Where the approach can fail

Code frequency is not a complete model of semantic normality: rare desired material may be altered, while an unwanted object can still use familiar codes. Both reconstruction paths retain the same top-level context.

05 / Next steps

Work in progress

Continuing comparative experiments, ablations, and manuscript refinement, with particular attention to evaluation protocols and failure cases.
