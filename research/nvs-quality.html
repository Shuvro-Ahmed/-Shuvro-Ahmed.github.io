Perceptual quality of novel-view-synthesis videos

Studying how local visual degradation and short low-quality intervals influence the agreement between objective quality metrics and human judgments of rendered videos.

Ongoing research2026–present
Novel-view synthesisHuman quality judgmentsSpatial + temporal analysis
01 / Question

What I’m investigating

To what extent can local and temporal pooling improve agreement between objective quality scores and human judgments of novel-view-synthesis videos?

02 / Approach

How the study is structured

The first stage compares conventional averaging with spatial-tail pooling and worst-window temporal pooling of existing quality metrics. Pooling settings are selected on training data rather than the test set. A second, exploratory stage considers a compact predictor using spatial image statistics, temporal signals such as optical flow or flicker, and optional learned features.

03 / Evaluation

What the evidence needs to show

The plan is to compare predictions with human quality ratings, using scene-disjoint evaluation to assess generalization and prevent views from the same scene leaking across training and testing. It also examines conditions where alternative pooling does not help.

04 / Limitations

Where the approach can fail

The study will test whether emphasizing local defects and short low-quality intervals helps consistently across scenes, and where conventional averaging remains preferable.

05 / Next steps

Work in progress

Implementing the pooling study, examining agreement with subjective ratings, and determining whether a learned predictor is justified by the evidence.
